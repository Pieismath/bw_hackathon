Paper Replication Machine
Build a system that takes a quantitative finance research paper as a PDF and the relevant dataset, and attempts to replicate the paper's key results end-to-end. The system reads the paper, extracts the methodology - what data is used, what transformations are applied, what model is estimated, what the key quantitative claims are — maps the paper's data requirements to the provided datasets, generates Python code that implements the analysis, executes it, and compares its output to the paper's claimed findings. Where results reproduce, the system confirms it. Where they diverge, it diagnoses why: was it a data difference, a methodological ambiguity the paper didn't fully specify, or a potential issue with the original results? The most valuable output isn't just
"did it replicate" — it's the structured assessment of where the paper was ambiguous, what assumptions the system made to proceed, and how sensitive the results are to those choices. Every research paper has gaps between what the authors did and what they wrote down. The system should surface those gaps explicitly rather than silently papering over them. An important dimension the system should evaluate is whether the paper's findings are practically tradeable — does the signal survive transaction costs, does it have capacity constraints, is the alpha theoretical or implementable? A paper that shows a statistically significant return premium in a backtest but requires trading illiquid microcaps at daily frequency is a different proposition than one that works in liquid large-cap equities at monthly rebalancing.
Motivation: Replicating published research is one of the most time-consuming steps in our investment process.
Before we can act on a paper's claims, we need to verify them — and that means reverse-engineering the methodology, wrangling data, writing code, and debugging discrepancies. This routinely takes days. A system that produces a credible first-pass replication in minutes gives researchers a massive head start and, critically, surfaces the assumptions and ambiguities that determine whether a paper's findings are actually useful in practice. The distinction between theoretical alpha and implementable alpha is something we care about deeply, and a good replication tool should force that question.
Starter Data: We will provide 2-3 pre-selected papers as PDFs chosen to be replicable in scope using the available data package. Matching data from FRED (macro series), defeatbeta/yahoo-finance-data (company fundamentals, prices, earnings), aufklarer/central-bank-communications (for policy-related papers), fancyzhx/ag_news (for news-based papers). Papers will be selected so the core result involves a data transformation and statistical test that can execute in minutes, not a training pipeline - but will contain real ambiguities the system needs to navigate.
